Producers & Distributors

Your harvest belongs on a lunch tray

From kale to cranberries to fresh-caught seafood, Massachusetts schools want to buy what you grow, raise, and catch. Whether you sell direct, through a distributor, or through a food hub, these tools can help you decide if school sales are right for your farm or food business — and get you started.

A steady market that invests in your community

Farm to school opens the door to an institutional market worth billions of dollars — and keeps federal school meal dollars circulating in the local economy.

Long-term

Reliable relationships

Many institutional buyers want to build long-term purchasing relationships with local farms — a dependable channel alongside your retail, CSA, or wholesale sales.

Money stays home

Buying from local producers creates jobs, strengthens the local economy, and helps keep Massachusetts land in farming.

New buyers

Funding for local food

Programs like N.E. Food for Schools have given MA districts millions in dedicated funds to purchase from Northeast farmers, fishers, and producers — with a priority on reaching historically underserved farmers.

Key resources for producers

Mass. Farm to School offers free tools to connect you with school buyers across the Commonwealth.

01

Add Your Business to Our Local Food Directory

The Local Food Directory (and its statewide map) is where food service directors go to find farmers and vendors interested in selling locally grown products to institutions. Listing your farm or fishing business is free — buyers can filter by product, from vegetables and orchard fruit to dairy, meat, eggs, seafood, honey, maple, and value-added products.

Submit your Farm Form
02

Explore the Harvest of the Month Program

Harvest of the Month is a statewide framework for local food procurement, promotion, and education that highlights a different locally grown crop every month of the school year. When schools feature the month's crop, they need local producers to supply it — a predictable, seasonal demand signal you can plan around.

Harvest of the Month
03

Sell through an aggregator

Not set up for direct school deliveries, invoicing, or light processing? Massachusetts food hubs aggregate products from many farms and handle the logistics of selling to schools and other institutions — a great fit for smaller farms or those new to wholesale.

Food Hub Listings page

Is selling to schools right for your business?

You don't need to answer yes to everything, but these questions, drawn from Mass. Farm to School's producer guidance can help you gauge whether the institutional market is a good fit today or a goal to grow toward.

01Do you already wholesale, or sell to distributors, stores, or other farmers?
02Can you communicate regularly with schools and track orders?
03Do you have the capacity to deliver, or arrange for deliveries?
04Do you have a diversified product list, or the ability to source from other local growers?
05Does the school calendar fit with your harvest or fishing season?
06Are you prepared to meet potential insurance or food safety requirements?
07Can you provide light processing for schools with limited kitchen equipment?

You don't have to figure this out alone

Mass. Farm to School staff offer free individual technical assistance to help farms and fishing businesses explore the school market, from responding to requests for quotes to connecting with the right buyers. They also host "Selling to Schools 101" webinars and workshops throughout the year.

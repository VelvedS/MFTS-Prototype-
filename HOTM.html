Harvest of the Month

Harvest of the Month is a statewide program that connects school cafeterias, classrooms, and communities with fresh, locally grown food. Each month we feature a Massachusetts grown or sourced food and provide recipes, classroom lessons, and hands-on learning activities that help students explore where their food comes from and why it matters.

How It Works

Harvest of the Month is a simple, flexible framework The program is designed to make it easy for schools and early education centers at every stage to participate, whether you're just starting out or looking to deepen a robust program.

01

Spotlight a Seasonal Crop

Each month of the school year is paired with a Massachusetts-grown crop — chosen based on what's fresh, available, and well-suited to the school cafeteria. Food service teams feature the crop in school meals at least twice a month, either as a part of a menu recipe, or by offering a taste test featuring the crop and where it was grown or caught, helping students make the connection between the food on their plate and where it comes from.

02

Bring Learning to Life

Educators receive a monthly newsletter with crop-specific classroom lessons, garden activities, hands-on cooking projects, and literacy and science tie-ins — all curriculum-aligned and ready to use. From strawberry taste tests to bean dissection to cranberry math, food becomes a real-world learning tool.

03

Connect Classroom and Community

Harvest of the Month works best when the whole school community shows up — students, families, teachers, food service staff, and local farmers. Taste tests, farm visits, school garden harvests, and newsletters for families all help build lasting connections to local food and the farms that grow it.

Participants receive access to a collection of resources designed to make local food education easy and engaging, including:

  • Classroom lessons aligned with Massachusetts standards
  • School meal promotion materials
  • Family newsletters and at-home activities
  • Recipes and taste test ideas
  • School garden connections
  • Recommended books at all grade levels

Whether you are serving thousands of school meals, teaching a classroom lesson, running an afterschool program, or engaging young children in a childcare setting, Harvest of the Month offers ready-to-use resources to help you bring local food into everyday learning.

Who It's For

Harvest of the Month is for everyone in the school community. Whether you're serving trays of food, teaching a lesson, or caring for the youngest learners, there's a version of Harvest of the Month built for you.

School Food Service Professionals

Food service directors and nutrition staff receive monthly resources to help source and feature Massachusetts-grown, caught, and raised crops on the cafeteria menu. We provide procurement guidance, supplier connections through our local food directory, and promotional materials to generate excitement in the cafeteria.

Nutrition resources & sign-up

Educators

Teachers receive monthly newsletters with crop-specific lessons, activities, and book recommendations designed for K–12 classrooms across subject areas. Lessons are grade-differentiated and tie food, nutrition, and agriculture into math, science, literacy, social studies, and more.

Educator sign-up

Early Education Providers

Early Education providers can sign up for a monthly newsletter to engage young learners through age-appropriate activities, books, songs, sensory exploration, and food experiences that build healthy habits from an early age.

Early education sign-up

Farmers & Producers

Massachusetts farms, fisheries, dairies, and food producers can connect directly with schools and early childhood programs looking for local food. List your farm or food business in our local food directory to connect with school food purchasers across the Commonwealth.

List your farm

Families

Harvest of the Month builds food curiosity that goes home with students. Families can extend the learning by trying the monthly crop at home, visiting local farms and farmers markets, reading the family newsletter, and exploring the MassGrown Map to find fresh food close to them.

Family resources

School Administrators

Building a school-wide Harvest of the Month program takes leadership. Administrators can integrate the program into school wellness policies, connect it with the school garden, and use it as a framework to deepen farm to school across the cafeteria, classroom, and community — the "3 C's" approach.

Administrator resources

Why It Works

More than a taste test. A school-wide shift.

Harvest of the Month works because it makes local food visible, familiar, and exciting — not just once, but month after month. When students see the same farmers showing up in the cafeteria and the classroom, when they grow the same bean in the school garden that appears on their lunch tray, something changes.

"The more successful farm to school programs are not 'add-ons' to school policies and curricula, but integrated throughout the school priorities and culture."
— Vermont FEED / VT Farm to School & Early Childhood Network

Research shows that repeated, positive exposure to local foods — through tasting, growing, cooking, and learning — builds healthier, more adventurous eaters. It also builds school meal participation, reduces plate waste, and deepens students' connection to the food system and the farmers who sustain it.

Harvest of the Month connects all three pillars of farm to school: cafeteria (fresh local food on the menu), classroom (crop-specific lessons and activities), and community (links to local farms, families, and food culture).

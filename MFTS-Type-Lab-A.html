Your lunch tray connects you to a farm

Every carrot, apple, and piece of lettuce on your school lunch tray has a story. Farm to school is about making those connections real and giving every student in Massachusetts a chance to be part of it.

What farm to school looks like for real students

Across Massachusetts, students are growing food, shaping menus, leading taste tests, and speaking up for better school meals. Here are some of their stories.

Codman Academy, Boston

Student Food Ambassadors testify before state legislators

Codman Academy's Food Ambassadors program gives 10th and 11th graders a two-year leadership role in food and nutrition at their school. Students have led community taste tests and testified before state legislators — bringing their own cafeteria experiences directly into the policy conversation.

Watertown Public Schools

High schoolers grow eight lettuce varieties in their own Freight Farm

Watertown high school students grow Red Oakleaf, Sweet Crisp Exponent, Red Gem Cocotine, and more inside a Freight Farm container on campus. Their lettuce ends up on the school salad bar — a direct line from student hands to student lunch trays. Lt. Governor Driscoll visited to celebrate the program in 2026.

Lenox Public Schools

Students grow herbs and greens hydroponically for school lunch

The Lenox food service team used a MA FRESH grant to install a Fork Farm hydroponic growing system. Students now grow fresh cilantro and salad greens in school — ingredients that show up on their own lunch trays, including in the school's award-winning fish taco recipe.

Dr. Franklin Perkins School, Lancaster

World Lunch Day brings global cuisines into the cafeteria

Students at Dr. Franklin Perkins explore cuisines from around the world through "World Lunch Day" — tasting Kwanzaa-inspired jerk chicken, Mexican pozole, and more, with ingredients sourced from local farms just miles from campus. Students take their cultural explorations seriously, passports in hand.

Northampton Public Schools

Freshampton: farm field trips, taste tests, and a Tray of the Year

Northampton's "Freshampton" program is one of the most celebrated farm to school efforts in the state. Students go on farm field trips, participate in regular cafeteria taste tests, and helped their school win the 2026 Terrific Tray of the Year — a statewide honor for outstanding school meals using local ingredients.

Ways for students to take action

Whether you're in elementary school or high school, there's a role for you in the farm to school movement — from growing food in your school garden to speaking up for better meals for all students.

All ages

Submit a Student Advocacy Form

Students of any age can advocate for farm to school by writing or drawing their responses to a short advocacy form — showing Massachusetts legislators what farm to school programs mean to real students. Ask your teacher to bring this into the classroom.

All ages

Start or join a school garden

Does your school have a garden? If not, it could. Massachusetts Farm to School has classroom and school garden resources for educators, and the MA FRESH grant program funds new growing spaces. Talk to your teacher or principal about getting one started.

All ages

Ask for a taste test in your cafeteria

Taste tests are one of the best ways for students to try new local foods and give their school nutrition team feedback on what they'd like to see on the menu. Ask your school's food service director to bring in a local ingredient for your class to try.

High school

Become a food leader at your school

Programs like Codman Academy's Food Ambassadors give students a formal leadership role in food and nutrition at their school — and the chance to bring that work to the state level. Talk to your school about starting a student food leadership program.

All ages

Celebrate Farm to School Month

Every October is Farm to School Month — a statewide celebration of local food, school gardens, and the farmers who grow food for Massachusetts kids. Schools across the Commonwealth host taste tests, farm field trips, and harvest events throughout the month.

All ages

Start a Composting Program

How families can support farm to school

Parents, guardians, and caregivers play a huge role in bringing farm to school to life — in your school community and at the state level. Here's where to start.

01

Talk to your school's nutrition team

Your school's food service director is often your best ally. Ask about where school meal ingredients come from, whether your school participates in the Harvest of the Month program, and whether they've applied for a MA FRESH grant.

02

Advocate for the FRESH Grant program

The MA FRESH Grant funds school gardens, taste tests, and local food purchasing for Massachusetts schools — but it needs to be made permanent. Join the MA Food for Massachusetts Kids coalition to tell your legislators why it matters.

Advocacy Coalition
03

Support your school's garden

Many school gardens rely on family volunteers for planting days, harvest events, and afterschool garden clubs. Contact your school's garden coordinator or PTA to find out how to get involved — or help get a garden started.

Garden Resources
04

Stay informed

Subscribe to the Massachusetts Farm to School newsletter to get farm to school news, program announcements, advocacy updates, and seasonal recipes delivered to your inbox.

Subscribe to the newsletter
05

Bring farm to school to your district

If your district doesn't yet have a strong farm to school program, the Massachusetts Farm to School Institute helps school teams build one. Talk to your principal or superintendent about applying for the next cohort.

Learn more
06

Vote for the Terrific Tray of the Year

Every spring, the public votes on the Terrific Tray of the Year — selecting the outstanding school meal from among the monthly winners. Cast your vote and share it with your family to support the school nutrition programs doing exceptional work.

Tell Massachusetts lawmakers farm to school matters

The Massachusetts Food for Massachusetts Kids Coalition is working to make the FRESH Grant program permanent — so every school in Massachusetts has the resources to start or expand farm to school. Students and families can help.

01

Look up your legislator

Visit malegislature.gov to find your state representative and senator by address.

02

Make an appointment

Call your legislator's office and ask to speak with the aide who covers agriculture, nutrition, or education.

03

Share your story

Tell them what farm to school means to you — your school garden, a favorite local meal, a taste test that changed your mind about a vegetable.

04

Ask for their support

Ask your legislator to support the bills that would make the FRESH Grant program permanent and create a local food incentive for schools.

Annual Summit · 2026
Conference November 14 & 15 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM UMass Amherst

Massachusetts Farm-to-School
Annual Summit 2026.

Two days. Three hundred and fifty people. Every part of the Massachusetts school food system in one room, working out what's next.

The Annual Summit is where MFTS gathers the people who make local food in schools possible. School nutrition directors trading procurement notes with the farmers shipping to them. Educators bringing classroom curriculum to the people who fund it. State agency staff sitting at the same lunch table as the third-graders piloting the recipes.

This year, we are at the UMass Amherst Campus Center for two full days of keynotes, breakout sessions, hands-on workshops, and a producer marketplace that takes over the entire main hall. Continental breakfast and a locally sourced lunch are included with registration on both days.

What to expect

The 2026 Summit is organized around four working tracks. You can mix and match across them, but most attendees pick a primary track and follow it through both days.

  • Procurement & food service. Practical sessions on contracts, MA FRESH grants, scratch-cook training, and supply forecasting from MA producers.
  • Classroom & garden curriculum. K-12 curriculum building, garden-based learning, and the Harvest of the Month program from the educator side.
  • Producers & supply. Selling to schools, distribution partnerships, and the producer marketplace floor.
  • Policy & advocacy. State legislation, district-level board work, and the path from a school garden to a statewide budget line.

Two keynotes

Friday morning opens with Dr. Yael Cohen on the public health case for scratch cooking in K-12 cafeterias, drawing on five years of district-level outcomes data from across New England. Saturday morning, we close with a roundtable of three Massachusetts school nutrition directors who took their districts from 4% local sourcing to over 30% in three school years.

Every Summit, I leave with three new procurement contacts and at least one recipe my kids will actually eat at lunch. That's the math. School Nutrition Director, Western MA, 2025 attendee

Who should come

The Summit is open to anyone working on Massachusetts farm-to-school: food service staff, classroom and garden educators, school administrators, farmers and producers, distributors, nonprofit partners, state agency staff, parents, and advocates. Educators receive professional development credit; food service directors can apply Summit hours toward continuing education requirements.

Registration closes November 1 or when capacity is reached, whichever comes first. Last year we hit capacity ten days before the deadline.

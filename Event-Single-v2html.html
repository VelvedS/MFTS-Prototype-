Farm-to-School Educator Workshop: Garden-Based Learning.

A two-and-a-half hour working session for K-12 educators on turning your school garden into a year-round outdoor classroom for math, ELA, life science, and food literacy.

VenueVirtual via Zoom
(link sent on registration)
CostFree
Registration required
12 spots remaining

What we'll cover

This workshop is built for the educator who has a garden plot (or wants one), wants to use it as a teaching surface, and needs a starting point that does not require a horticulture degree. We work in small breakout rooms with a facilitator and live demo of curriculum.

  • A 5-minute mental model for mapping your existing standards onto garden activities.
  • Three ready-to-run lesson plans for K-2, 3-5, and 6-8 that we have piloted in MA schools this past year.
  • How to time garden curriculum to the MA growing season so cold-weather months are productive too.
  • Working with food service: getting your harvest onto a cafeteria tray, even occasionally.
  • The "winter classroom": indoor sprouting, seed saving, and herb walls that keep the garden alive November through March.
You'll leave with

A workbook with all three pilot lesson plans (downloadable PDFs), a planning template for mapping your standards to the garden calendar, and a private Slack channel for follow-up with the cohort.

Who it's for

Classroom teachers (K-8 most common), garden coordinators, after-school program leads, and curriculum specialists. No previous garden experience required. School administrators are welcome to join in observation mode.

Format

Two and a half hours on Zoom. Forty-five minutes of group session, three rotating breakout rooms (fifteen minutes each), a working break, and a final question-and-answer period with the facilitators. Sessions are recorded and shared with registrants only.

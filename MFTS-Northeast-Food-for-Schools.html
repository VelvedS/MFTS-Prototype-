Northeast Food for Schools Challenge

Investing $6 million in local food for Massachusetts schools

The Northeast Food for Schools Challenge is a call to action for Massachusetts school nutrition programs — building on an original federal investment to keep local farms on school lunch trays, even as federal funding has been cut.

Join the Challenge

Your school's pledge is part of something bigger

Each School Food Authority (SFA) that joins the NFS Challenge pledges to spend the dollar value of the federal funding they would have received — on Massachusetts-grown and regionally produced foods — by the end of 2026.

Eligible foods include unprocessed or minimally processed fruits, vegetables, dairy, grains, meat, and seafood grown, raised, or harvested in New England or New York.

Participating schools receive access to FoodTrails, a purchase tracking platform funded by DESE, as well as procurement support from Massachusetts Farm to School and recognition on a public leaderboard. Registration is first-come, first-served through SY2025–26.

How we got here

From a federal program to a state-led challenge

The Northeast Food for Schools Challenge didn't emerge from nothing. It was built on the foundation — and in direct response to the sudden loss — of a successful federal program that transformed how Massachusetts schools buy food.

01 December 2021

The Local Food for Schools Program launches

The USDA announced an ambitious new initiative: the Local Food for Schools (LFS) Cooperative Agreement Program, providing $200 million nationally to help schools purchase locally grown and raised products. The program was designed to rebuild supply chains strained by the COVID-19 pandemic while getting fresh, regional food into school cafeterias.

Massachusetts received $3.5 million — funds that flowed to school districts across the Commonwealth from September 2023 through December 2024. Massachusetts Farm to School served as the administrative partner, connecting schools to local suppliers and processing reimbursement requests.

02
March 2025

Federal funding is abruptly terminated

In early March 2025, the USDA notified states that the Local Food for Schools program — along with the Local Food Purchase Assistance program — would be terminated. The two programs combined had earmarked over $1 billion nationally for the fiscal year. States, school districts, and farmers were given 60 days' notice.

The termination letter cited that the programs "no longer effectuate the priorities of the administration." Farmers who had already planned their seasons around the contracts, purchased seed, and booked processing time were left scrambling. In Massachusetts, local orchards and food hubs that had built reliable school partnerships faced sudden uncertainty.

03 Late 2025

Massachusetts responds with a state-led challenge

Rather than accept the loss, Massachusetts DESE's Office of Food and Nutrition Programs — partnering with Massachusetts Farm to School — launched the Northeast Food for Schools (NFS) Challenge. The $6 million collective goal represents the exact amount originally earmarked for Massachusetts schools through the terminated federal program for SY2025–26.

Instead of top-down grants, the Challenge asks each School Food Authority to pledge their own share of local food spending and track it through a new public platform — turning individual commitments into a statewide movement.

Program Snapshots

Local food, on real trays

Northampton: A Model District

Northampton Public Schools, the home of the "Freshampton" nutrition program, became a showcase for what LFS funds could accomplish. Their team built relationships with local food hubs and farmers to feature local apples, lettuce, squash, potatoes, and carrots on school menus. A kale Caesar salad taste test won over students who "did not think they would like it." Northampton won the 2026 statewide Terrific Tray of the Year award for its commitment to locally sourced, universally free school meals.

Carlson Orchards & the Worcester Food Hub

For farmers like Katie Carlson of Carlson Orchards in Harvard, MA, the Local Food for Schools program created reliable, predictable school demand through food hubs like the Worcester Regional Food Hub. The termination of that federal program put those relationships at risk, and is exactly the kind of farm-school partnership the NFS Challenge is designed to protect and continue.

What is Farm to School?

Farm to school connects kids with fresh, locally grown food and the farmers who grow it — turning cafeterias, classrooms, and communities into living lessons about where food comes from and why it matters.

The Basics

A win for kids, farmers, and communities.

Farm to school is a set of programs and practices that bring local food, food education, and agricultural experiences into schools and early childhood settings. It encompasses everything from sourcing produce from nearby farms for school meals to maintaining school gardens, incorporating nutrition and food system education into the classroom, and farm field trips. At its heart, farm to school builds relationships between children and their food, between schools and farmers, and between communities and their local food systems.

The 3 C’s Framework

Vermont FEED's 3 C's model, developed in 2000 and now used nationwide, is built on the idea that lasting farm to school programs must connect three areas of school life:

01

Cafeteria

Fresh, local food from nearby farms, fisheries, and producers served in school meals. Procurement relationships support farms and provide food service departments seasonal, fresh ingredients for the school community.

02

Classroom

Food systems education built into the curriculum, from ELA and history to science and math. School gardens become outdoor classrooms. Cooking lessons become hands-on science. Farm field trips become geography and ecology come to life.

03

Community

Families, farmers, food producers, and neighbors all play a role. Farm to school strengthens local food systems, builds economic resilience, and creates shared community identity around food and agriculture.

Farm to School Takes Many Shapes

From the cafeteria to the garden to the classroom, farm to school activities meet kids and educators where they are. Successful programs aren't add-ons to an already crowded schedule — they're woven into the culture of a school.

01

Local Food Procurement

School nutrition programs source fruits, vegetables, grains, dairy, meat, and seafood from nearby farms and producers, integrating fresh, seasonal ingredients into everyday school meals.

02

School Gardens

Outdoor and indoor growing spaces where students can plant, tend, harvest, and cook what they grow. Gardens become living laboratories for science, math, language arts, and nutrition education.

03

Food & Nutrition Education

Curriculum about food systems, nutrition, cooking, agriculture, and the environment that is aligned with learning standards and designed to be engaging and hands-on.

04

Farm Field Trips

Students visit working farms, orchards, fisheries, and food processors to see firsthand how food is grown and raised. Farmers also visit classrooms to share their expertise and stories.

05

Taste Tests & Cooking

Students sample new foods, especially seasonal local produce they may never have tried, and participate in cooking activities that build confidence, curiosity, and culinary skills.

06

Early Childhood Programs

Farm to school extends beyond K–12. Childcare centers, preschools, Head Start programs all offer opportunities to establish healthy food relationships from the earliest ages.

Equity & Access

Farm to school must be grounded in equity.

Access to fresh, nourishing food is a basic human right. That's why leading farm to school organizations — from the National Farm to School Network to Vermont FEED to Massachusetts Farm to School — center equity as a core value of the movement.

A racially just food system requires that farm to school programs are designed with and for all communities — including communities of color, low-income communities, rural and urban communities alike. The benefits of farm to school should not be reserved for the privileged few.

This means intentional work: ensuring that local food procurement reaches under-resourced schools, that education is culturally relevant and inclusive, that farmers from all backgrounds have access to school markets, and that every child — regardless of zip code — has the opportunity to grow, cook, and eat good food.
